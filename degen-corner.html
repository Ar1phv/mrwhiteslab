01 · WHAT "DEGEN" ACTUALLY MEANS

Not an insult. A risk category you choose to enter.

In this industry, degen describes trading or allocating capital into the highest risk, least proven end of the market, new memecoins, unaudited launches, high leverage positions, on the understanding that the odds are bad and the potential loss is total. It's used as a badge, not a slur, people say it about themselves openly.

The useful distinction isn't whether degen activity is good or bad, it's whether the person doing it actually understands they've left the investing side of this industry entirely and entered something closer to a lottery ticket with better production values. Most of the damage in this corner of the market happens to people who didn't realize that's the game they were actually playing.

02 · MEMECOIN MECHANICS

A joke with a bonding curve attached.

A memecoin is a token with no stated utility, no product, no roadmap, its entire value proposition is attention and momentum. Most launch on a bonding curve, an automated pricing mechanism where the token's price rises mechanically as more people buy, with no presale, no team allocation in the simplest versions, anyone can buy from the first block.

Diagram · A Bonding Curve Launch

Price is mechanical, not based on any product. The earliest buyers on the flattest part of the curve have the most room below them. The latest buyers have almost none.

Once the curve fully sells out, many of these tokens migrate their liquidity to a standard decentralized exchange from Part 05, where normal AMM trading takes over. The overwhelming majority of tokens launched this way never reach that migration point at all, momentum dies, buyers stop coming, and the price simply sits at whatever point it stalled, permanently.

03 · AIRDROP FARMING

Doing real work for a reward that may never arrive.

Airdrop farming means using a protocol repeatedly, specifically because the team has hinted or confirmed a future token reward for early users, in hopes of qualifying for a meaningful allocation once it launches. This is a real, legitimate way people have earned significant value in this industry, several large token launches rewarded early users generously.

Protocols actively defend against sybil farming, one person running hundreds of wallets to multiply their expected reward, using wallet clustering analysis and behavior pattern detection to disqualify accounts that look coordinated rather than organic. Getting flagged as a sybil farmer typically means zero allocation, after months of real gas fees spent trying to qualify.

There is no guarantee any given protocol ever launches a token at all, or that the reward is worth more than the gas fees and time spent farming it. Treat the gas fees and time as the real, certain cost, and the airdrop itself as a maybe, never the reverse.

04 · LEVERAGE CULTURE

Perpetuals from Part 08, pushed to the edge on purpose.

High leverage trading, often 20x, 50x, sometimes 100x on perpetual contracts covered in Part 08, multiplies both gains and losses by that same factor. At high leverage, a move of just one or two percent against your position can trigger a full liquidation, your entire margin gone, not a partial loss.

Diagram · Liquidation Distance At Different Leverage

Bar height is roughly how far price can move against you before liquidation. At 100x, a move smaller than the normal noise of most assets wipes the position.

Ordinary market volatility, the kind that happens constantly and means nothing on a spot chart, is frequently larger than the liquidation distance at these leverage levels. This isn't a tail risk you might encounter, it's closer to a near certainty over enough attempts, which is why sustained high leverage trading has one of the worst long run survival rates of anything covered on this entire site.

05 · SNIPING NEW LAUNCHES

Trying to be first, against bots built to always be first instead.

Sniping means buying a new token in the first seconds or even the first block after it launches, trying to capture the lowest possible point on the bonding curve or earliest price. Manual snipers compete directly against automated sniping bots that submit transactions faster than any human can click, frequently paying extra priority fees specifically to win that first block, the same MEV dynamic covered in Part 08.

A meaningful share of new launches are also structured deliberately to extract value from snipers specifically, a team holding a large early allocation that dumps directly into the first wave of buyers the moment real demand shows up. Being fast doesn't protect you from being the exit liquidity for someone who was faster, and had a plan you couldn't see.

06 · RED FLAGS ON A NEW LAUNCH

The checklist from Part 10, run at actual launch speed.

→
Check the holder distribution immediately. A handful of wallets holding most of the supply this early is a direct setup for the dump described above.
→
Check if liquidity is actually locked. The exact rug pull mechanic from Part 10, now happening in minutes instead of months.
→
Check for a working, honest contract. Unlimited mint functions or hidden transfer taxes are common on fast, low effort launches specifically built to extract from degen flow.
⨯
Never buy based on urgency alone. "Only going up from here" and "last chance before migration" are the exact phrases the setup from Part 09's scam section is built around.
07 · THE MATH NOBODY POSTS

You only see the screenshots of the trades that worked.

Survivorship bias is the reason degen trading looks more winnable on social media than it actually is. Someone who turned five hundred dollars into fifty thousand posts that screenshot everywhere. The hundreds of people who made the same kind of bet and lost it all post nothing, they simply go quiet. The visible sample you're seeing is not a representative sample, it's filtered down to almost nothing but the outcomes that worked.

Expected Value, Stated Plainly Expected value = (probability of winning × size of win) − (probability of losing × size of loss)
A trade with a small win chance and a total loss on the downside needs an enormous payout on the rare win just to break even over many attempts, let alone turn a real long run profit.

None of this means nobody ever wins in this corner of the market. People genuinely do. It means the honest way to think about it is closer to buying a lottery ticket you understand the odds of, not an investment you're executing a strategy on.

08 · IF YOU'RE GOING TO DO THIS ANYWAY

The same discipline from the TA Corner, just stricter here.

If you choose to participate in this corner of the market with full awareness of the odds above, the position sizing rule from the TA Corner gets significantly stricter, not looser. Size every degen trade as money you've already mentally accepted as spent the moment you commit it, never money earmarked for anything that actually matters to your life.

→
Never use leverage or borrowed capital for degen plays. The leverage math from this page and total loss risk from memecoins compound into something far worse than either alone.
→
Set a fixed, small, separate budget for this category only. Never top it up mid session chasing a loss, the exact revenge trading pattern covered in the TA Corner.
→
Take real profit off the table when a position actually works. Moving the original stake back out once it's recovered means the rest is genuinely free risk, not just a number on a screen you're still afraid to lose.

The discipline covered across the TA Corner, position sizing, risk to reward, a trade journal, applies here with more force, not less. This is the highest variance corner of the entire site. Treat it with the most caution, not the least.

Everyone posts the trade that worked. Almost nobody posts the ten that didn't. Do the math before you decide which post you're likely to be.
