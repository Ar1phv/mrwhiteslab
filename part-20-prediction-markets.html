Prediction Markets

Where information itself becomes the tradeable asset.

Twenty parts, one thread running through all of them. Markets price things. This last one prices the thing every other market secretly depends on, being right about the future. Fitting place to end.

01 · HOW A PREDICTION MARKET ACTUALLY WORKS

Betting on an outcome, priced as a probability.

A prediction market lets users trade shares tied to the outcome of a specific future event, an election, a rate decision, whether a protocol ships a feature by a stated date. A share priced at 70 cents on a dollar effectively represents the market's collective estimate that the outcome has roughly a 70 percent chance of happening, information genuinely useful independent of whether you ever place a trade yourself.

On chain versions, Polymarket being the clearest current example, run this through the same smart contract settlement mechanics covered throughout this guide, no bookmaker setting the odds, the market itself sets the price through the same supply and demand mechanics from Part 05's AMMs and Part 08's order books.

02 · WHY THE PRICE IS OFTEN MORE ACCURATE THAN THE EXPERTS

Money attached to an opinion changes the opinion's quality.

The wisdom of crowds effect is well documented past crypto entirely, aggregated predictions from many independent, incentivized participants frequently outperform individual expert forecasts, because real financial stakes filter out low conviction noise in a way a casual opinion poll never does. Prediction markets are this effect turned into permanent, transparent infrastructure, running continuously instead of as a one off academic study.

This is the actual utility, past the speculation. A liquid prediction market on a real world event is often a better real time forecast than any single pundit or poll, because everyone with a financial stake in being right is actively correcting the price, all the time, in public.

03 · RESOLUTION, THE HARDEST PROBLEM IN THE WHOLE DESIGN

Who decides what actually happened.

A prediction market's entire integrity depends on how it resolves, determining and confirming which outcome actually occurred, so contracts can settle correctly. This is the oracle problem from Part 08 in its purest form, a real world event has to get reported onto the chain accurately, and disputed resolutions, ambiguous event wording, or a compromised resolution source can undermine an otherwise well designed market entirely.

Some platforms use decentralized dispute mechanisms, a token holder vote resolving contested outcomes. Others lean on a more centralized resolution source. Check a market's resolution mechanism before trusting a large position to it, the same way Part 08 said to check an oracle before trusting a perpetual position to it.

Every market in this guide prices something. This one prices the future itself, and the price is usually more honest than the headlines arguing about it.
