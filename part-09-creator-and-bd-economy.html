The Creator And BD Economy

How attention actually converts into protocol growth, and what separates a real campaign from noise.

Everything before this part you could learn from documentation. This part you can't. It comes from running Outpost, pitching dozens of protocols directly, and watching which campaigns actually moved users and which ones just moved impressions. Two very different numbers.

01 · WHAT A KOL ACTUALLY IS, PAST THE BUZZWORD

Rented trust, not rented reach.

A KOL, key opinion leader, sells access to an audience that already trusts them. Protocols pay for that trust transfer, not for the raw follower count. This is why a smaller creator with an engaged, relevant audience routinely outperforms a large account with passive followers who scroll past everything.

Vetting a creator means checking engagement quality against their audience, not their headline follower number. Comment quality, reply ratio, whether their past sponsored content actually drove wallet connections or just likes. Outpost exists specifically because most protocols don't have the time or internal expertise to do this vetting properly, and most creators can't prove their real value without a broker translating it.

02 · INSIGHT FIRST, OFFER SECOND

The pitch framework that actually gets replies.

Cold outreach in this industry is saturated. Every founder gets a dozen generic "we'd love to partner" messages a day, and every one of them gets ignored. The framework that works, the one I run at Outpost, is simple to state and hard to execute. Lead with a specific, correct insight about the protocol's current gap, before you ever mention what you're offering.

Show the founder you actually looked at their product before you show them a price sheet. A gap analysis on their current growth funnel, a specific weakness in their creator mix, a concrete number pulled from their own public metrics. That's the first message. The offer comes after they've already registered you as someone who did the work.

This is slower than mass outreach. It also converts at a completely different rate, because you're not competing with the other eleven messages in their inbox that all read the same.

03 · WHAT A REAL CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE LOOKS LIKE

Milestones, not vibes.

A real creator campaign has a defined length, usually measured in weeks, a specific creator roster matched to the protocol's actual audience, and a content calendar tied to real product moments, not just a stream of generic posts. Payment structured against milestones, not a flat retainer with no accountability attached, protects both sides.

Referral tracking has to be transparent from day one. I've had to resolve exactly this kind of dispute directly, a creator's referral attribution getting questioned mid campaign. The fix isn't a better excuse after the fact, it's building the tracking system correctly before the campaign launches, so the question never comes up.

04 · AMBASSADOR PROGRAMS, READ THE STRUCTURE BEFORE YOU JOIN

Most ambassador programs pay in exposure. Read that as a warning.

Having evaluated more of these than I can count, the pattern is consistent. A program worth your time has a clear, published compensation structure, defined deliverables, and a track record of actually paying past participants on time. A program that's vague about payment but detailed about "amazing opportunity" and "early access" is optimizing for free labor, not for a real partnership.

→
Check for a published payment structure. Specific numbers, specific timelines. Vague promises are a red flag on their own.
→
Ask past participants directly. Every legitimate program has ambassadors willing to confirm they actually got paid.
→
Weigh passive value honestly. Some programs offer real long term positioning even without immediate cash. Know which trade you're actually making.
05 · THE OPERATOR RED FLAGS I ACTUALLY WALKED AWAY FROM

Evaluating this market from the inside means seeing the fraud early.

Running BD at volume means encountering fake opportunities constantly, alongside the real ones. The pattern that consistently precedes a scam. Urgency with no substance, pressure to commit before you've seen real product or real terms, compensation structures that only make sense if new participants keep funding old ones, and server or project setups with permissions that don't match what a legitimate operation would need.

I've declined and exited multiple opportunities that hit these markers directly, before they cost me anything. The instinct to trust a good pitch is exactly what these setups are built to exploit. Slow down, verify independently, and never let deal size or urgency override that check.

Attention is rented trust. Spend it carelessly and the audience stops lending it to you.
