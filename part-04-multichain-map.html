THE GUIDE · PART FOUR

The Multichain Map

Where each major chain actually wins, judged against the framework from Part 03.

You now know consensus, finality, and the trilemma. Time to apply it. Six ecosystems, judged on what they were actually built to do, not what their marketing claims. No chain wins everywhere. Anyone telling you otherwise is selling, not informing.

ETHEREUM·SOLANA· INJECTIVE·XRPL· CARDANO·BITCOIN L2· ETHEREUM·SOLANA· INJECTIVE·XRPL
ETHEREUM 01 · THE SETTLEMENT LAYER EVERYTHING ELSE MEASURES AGAINST

Slow, expensive, and still the default court of final judgment.

Ethereum runs Proof of Stake, secured by hundreds of thousands of independent validators, the widest decentralization of any programmable chain by a large margin. That's the tradeoff it made deliberately. Base layer throughput is modest, gas can spike hard during demand, and it leans on a growing stack of Layer 2 rollups to handle volume it can't process directly.

What Ethereum actually sells is not speed. It sells the deepest liquidity, the widest developer base, and the strongest claim to being the chain institutions default to when they need maximum credible neutrality. If a protocol needs to be trusted by parties who trust nothing else, this is still where it settles.

SOLANA 02 · THROUGHPUT AS THE ENTIRE THESIS

Built for speed first, everything else second.

Solana runs a single, tightly coupled Layer 1 with a unique consensus innovation, Proof of History, a verifiable clock that lets validators agree on transaction order without waiting on each other constantly. The result is thousands of transactions per second and fees measured in fractions of a cent, no Layer 2 required.

The tradeoff sits in validator hardware requirements, running a competitive validator takes real infrastructure, which narrows who can realistically participate compared to Ethereum's lighter requirements. Solana leans toward the throughput and scale corner of the trilemma, deliberately, and it shows in what gets built there, high frequency trading apps, consumer scale NFT drops, anything that dies under Ethereum's base layer fees.

INJECTIVE 03 · BUILT FOR TRADING, NOT RETROFITTED FOR IT

A chain designed around the order book, not the wallet.

Injective is a Proof of Stake chain purpose built for finance, with a native on chain order book baked into the protocol itself, rather than bolted on as an application. Near zero gas fees, fast finality, and Native EVM support that lets Ethereum tooling deploy directly, without the usual bridge friction.

This is the chain I've spent the most operational time inside, running MARK Protocol here and working the ecosystem through Outpost. What it wins at is specific, derivatives, structured products, anything where execution speed and fee cost directly determine whether a strategy is even viable. It's not trying to be everything. It's trying to be the best possible venue for one category of activity, and largely succeeding.

XRPL 04 · THE OLDEST WORKING PAYMENTS RAIL IN THIS INDUSTRY

Built in 2012, before most of this industry had a name.

The XRP Ledger runs its own consensus protocol, not Proof of Work or classic Proof of Stake, but a federated Byzantine agreement model where a trusted set of validators reach consensus without mining or staking. Settlement finality lands in seconds, and it was purpose built from day one for one job, moving value across borders cheaply.

The criticism is real, the validator set has historically leaned more curated than fully open compared to Solana or Ethereum. The result, in exchange, is a chain that has quietly processed payments reliably for over a decade while newer chains were still solving basic uptime. Judge it on what it's for, cross border settlement, not on whether it's the most decentralized chain in the room.

CARDANO 05 · RESEARCH FIRST, SHIP SECOND, ON PURPOSE

The chain that moves slow because it refuses to skip peer review.

Cardano runs Ouroboros, a peer reviewed Proof of Stake protocol, built through an academic research process rather than the move fast and iterate approach most chains take. Every major upgrade goes through formal verification before shipping. This is the most deliberate tradeoff on this list, development speed sacrificed directly for correctness and provable security.

Ecosystem activity has historically lagged Ethereum and Solana in raw volume, partly a direct consequence of that caution. But for builders who prioritize formal guarantees over speed to market, particularly in regions and use cases where a bug is not an acceptable cost of iteration, that tradeoff is the entire appeal, not a weakness.

BITCOIN L2S 06 · GIVING THE OLDEST CHAIN NEW JOBS

Bitcoin secures. The layers on top do the work.

Bitcoin itself was never built for programmability, no general smart contracts, deliberately minimal by design. A growing set of Layer 2 networks now build on top of it, using various models, sidechains, rollup style constructions, and the Lightning Network for instant small payments, to add functionality while still settling back to Bitcoin's base security.

This is Bitcoin's answer to the trilemma without changing its own base layer at all. Push the scaling and programmability tradeoffs up a layer, keep the base chain exactly as conservative and battle tested as it's always been. Evaluate each Bitcoin L2 on its own trust assumptions, some inherit Bitcoin's security closely, others lean more on their own validator set than the marketing implies.

Chain choice is not loyalty. It's matching the tool to the job in front of you.
