Derivatives
Beyond Perps

Options, structured products, and on chain insurance. The layer past the perpetual contract.

Part 08 covered perpetuals, the dominant on chain derivative by volume. It's not the only one. Options, structured yield products, and insurance are smaller markets on chain, growing steadily, and worth understanding before you assume perps are the whole story.

01 · OPTIONS, THE RIGHT WITHOUT THE OBLIGATION

Pay a premium for the choice, not the commitment.

An option gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a set strike price before a set expiry date. A call option profits if the asset rises above the strike, a put profits if it falls below it. The buyer pays a premium upfront for that right, the maximum they can lose, while the seller collects that premium but carries open ended risk if the market moves hard against the position.

On chain options work through the same smart contract mechanics as everything else in this guide, no clearinghouse, the contract itself enforces settlement automatically at expiry based on the final price an oracle reports, the same oracle dependency flagged in Part 08. Liquidity here is meaningfully thinner than perpetual markets, which means wider spreads and more slippage on larger size.

02 · STRUCTURED PRODUCTS, AUTOMATED STRATEGIES IN A WRAPPER

Someone else runs the options strategy. You just deposit.

A structured product vault packages a specific derivatives strategy, most commonly selling covered calls or cash secured puts, into a single deposit product. Depositors contribute an asset, the vault's smart contract automatically executes the options strategy on a set schedule, and yield gets distributed back based on the premiums collected.

Read this exactly like the yield discussion from Part 05. The advertised return is real premium income, not an emissions subsidy, which is a genuine improvement over a lot of DeFi yield. It's also not risk free, a large adverse price move can produce a real loss on the underlying position that the premium income doesn't fully offset. Understand the specific strategy running inside the vault, not just the headline number.

03 · ON CHAIN INSURANCE, COVERING THE RISKS FROM PART 10

A market for insuring against the exact failures already covered in this guide.

On chain insurance protocols let users pay a premium to purchase cover against specific risks, most commonly a smart contract exploit draining a protocol you have funds deposited in, directly addressing the exact exploit categories from Part 10. Other members of the protocol act as underwriters, staking capital that pays out claims in exchange for a share of the premiums collected.

This only works if claims are assessed fairly and paid reliably, which has historically been the hard part, some protocols use community voting to adjudicate claims, others use a more centralized claims assessment team. Check a protocol's actual claims payout history, not just its total value covered, before trusting it to protect a real position.

04 · WHY THIS LAYER MATTERS EVEN IF YOU NEVER TRADE IT

Derivatives are how a market prices its own uncertainty.

Options pricing reflects the market's collective expectation of future volatility, information that's genuinely useful even if you never buy or sell an option yourself. Elevated options premiums across an asset are the market pricing in real uncertainty ahead, a signal worth reading alongside the spot price itself. This is the same reason professional traders watch derivatives markets even when their own positions are entirely spot.

Perps get the volume. Options, vaults, and insurance are where the market actually prices its own risk.
