Web3 Gaming
And SocialFi

Where token incentives meet entertainment and social platforms, and why most of the first wave failed.

Two categories, one shared lesson. Both promised to fuse crypto incentives directly into games and social platforms people already loved. The first wave mostly failed, for a specific, avoidable reason this part actually names.

PLAY TO EARN·IN-GAME ASSET· CREATOR TOKEN·ENGAGEMENT MINING· SUSTAINABILITY· PLAY TO EARN·CREATOR TOKEN
01 · PLAY TO EARN, THE FIRST WAVE AND WHY IT BROKE

Paying players in a token nobody outside the game wanted.

Play to earn games reward players with tokens or NFT assets for in game activity, tradeable and cashable outside the game itself. Axie Infinity was the breakout example in 2021, players in the Philippines and elsewhere earning meaningful real income from gameplay at its peak, a genuinely novel outcome worth taking seriously.

The collapse followed the exact yield sustainability pattern from Part 05. New player deposits funded existing player rewards, not real external revenue, a structure that only holds while the player base keeps growing. When new player growth slowed, the token price collapsed, and the entire earn loop broke for everyone still playing. The game mechanic was real. The token economy underneath it wasn't sustainable, and those are two separate questions that got conflated at the peak of the hype.

02 · THE SECOND WAVE, FUN FIRST, TOKEN SECOND

Learning from the exact failure above.

Newer web3 games are increasingly built with actual gameplay as the primary product, and on chain ownership of in game assets as a secondary feature layered on top, rather than the entire pitch. This flips the Axie Infinity order of operations. A game people would play without any token attached, with genuine on chain asset ownership added, has a real chance of surviving a market downturn that a pure earn mechanic never could.

The tell to check for, would this game have any players if the token dropped to zero tomorrow. If the honest answer is no, you're looking at a yield farm wearing a game's skin, the exact pattern from Part 05, just reskinned.

03 · SOCIALFI, TOKENIZING ATTENTION DIRECTLY

Turning follower engagement into a tradeable asset.

SocialFi platforms let creators or their content directly tie to a tradeable token or NFT, friend.tech's key mechanic being the clearest example, where owning a creator's "keys" grants access to their private chat, with the keys themselves trading on a bonding curve tied to demand. This directly monetizes what Part 09 called rented trust, except now the trust itself becomes the tradeable asset instead of just the campaign built on top of it.

The same sustainability question applies here as everywhere else in this part. Early SocialFi platforms saw huge initial volume followed by steep drop offs once the novelty faded and the underlying utility, in most cases, didn't extend much past speculation on the token itself. Watch for platforms building real retained utility past the initial trading frenzy, that's the signal separating a real product from a temporary mechanic.

Fun and utility have to come first. The token is the reward layer, never the whole product.
