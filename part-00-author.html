FOUNDATION

Architecture taught me structure first.

Final year architecture student, Bayero University Kano. Before I ever touched a smart contract, I was trained to think in load, structure, and constraint. A building that looks good and fails under its own weight is a failure, no matter how the render looked. That standard never left me.

Web3 is full of things that look good and fail under their own weight. Tokenomics with no floor. Protocols with no real users behind the volume. I look at a whitepaper the way I look at a structural drawing. Where's the load bearing wall. What happens when the pressure hits it.

BUILDER

Then I started building the load bearing parts myself.

MARK Protocol. A soulbound creator support system on Injective Native EVM. Non transferable by design, EIP-5192 standard, because reputation should not be a token you can buy. Full contract suite, tested, shipped as real infrastructure, not a deck.

Verity. Co-founded with a technical partner who built Merlin Markets on Injective. A conviction scoring oracle for DeFi, currently in the Euclid accelerator. We're not a lender. We're the scoring layer other lenders plug into. Different business, different discipline.

Building forces honesty. You can't market your way out of a failing contract.

OPERATOR

And I learned the market by working it directly.

I run Outpost, a creator distribution brokerage connecting vetted KOLs with protocol growth campaigns across Injective, Solana, XRPL, and Cardano. Before Outpost had a name, I was cold pitching protocols directly, ISLE Finance, XORA, Dexless, Morfi, and dozens more, learning what actually converts attention into users versus what just generates noise.

That's the seat I write from. Not a trader posting screenshots. Not an influencer reading headlines. Someone who has sat across from founders, negotiated the terms, and shipped the contracts.

Three disciplines, one standard: does it hold weight, or does it just look like it does.
