Trading And Market Structure

How price actually forms on chain, and what changes when there's no exchange in the middle.

Trading on chain looks similar to trading on a centralized exchange from the outside, a chart, an order button, a fill. Underneath, almost everything about how the trade actually executes is different. That difference is where both the opportunity and the risk live.

01 · CUSTODY IS THE REAL DIFFERENCE

Same chart. Completely different risk underneath it.

A CEX, centralized exchange, holds your funds in custody while you trade, matching orders internally, off chain, and only touching the blockchain when you deposit or withdraw. Fast, familiar, and entirely dependent on the exchange's solvency and honesty, a dependency that has failed catastrophically before.

On chain trading settles every transaction directly on the ledger, no intermediary holding your funds between trades. You keep custody the entire time. The tradeoff is that every trade now costs gas, moves at the chain's speed, and is visible to anyone watching the mempool before it's even confirmed.

Centralized

Fast, deep liquidity, custody risk, dependent on the exchange staying solvent and honest.

On Chain

Self custodied the entire time, fully transparent, exposed to gas costs and public mempool visibility.

02 · MEV, THE TAX YOU DON'T SEE COMING

Someone can see your trade before it lands.

Before your on chain transaction is confirmed, it sits briefly in a public queue called the mempool, visible to anyone running the right software. This visibility created an entire discipline called MEV, maximal extractable value, where bots detect a pending trade and insert their own transactions immediately before and after it to profit from the price movement your trade is about to cause.

This isn't hacking, it's exploiting a structural feature of public, ordered transactions. Some chains and tools now offer private transaction submission specifically to reduce this exposure. Know whether the chain or wallet you're using has any MEV protection before executing large trades.

03 · PERPETUALS AND LEVERAGE ON CHAIN

Borrowed size, no expiry date, and an oracle deciding your fate.

A perpetual contract lets you take a leveraged long or short position on an asset without an expiry date, unlike traditional futures. On chain perps rely on a funding rate mechanism, a periodic payment between long and short holders, to keep the contract's price tethered to the actual spot price.

These contracts depend entirely on an oracle, a service feeding real world price data onto the chain, because the blockchain itself has no native awareness of external prices. A manipulated or lagging oracle has been the root cause of real exploits and unfair liquidations. Chains built specifically for trading, Injective among the clearest examples, invest heavily in oracle reliability and speed for exactly this reason, because the entire product fails if the price feed can't be trusted.

04 · LIQUIDITY DEPTH, THE NUMBER THAT ACTUALLY PROTECTS YOU

Price only means something if you can actually get it.

A displayed price is meaningless without checking how much size can trade at or near it. Thin books and shallow pools mean your own order moves the price against you before it fully fills, the slippage mechanic from Part 05, now applied to leveraged and larger size trades where it hurts considerably more.

Before sizing any position, check depth at your intended size, not just the headline price. This single habit prevents more losses than any indicator or strategy.

On chain, the price you see and the price you get are two different questions. Always check both.
