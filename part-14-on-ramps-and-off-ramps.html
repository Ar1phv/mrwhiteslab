On-Ramps And
Off-Ramps

The first real wall every newcomer hits, and the one thing most guides assume you've already solved.

Every part before this one assumed you already had crypto in a wallet. Fair assumption for twelve parts of mechanism. Not fair for a complete guide. Getting money in, and getting it back out, is its own skill, with its own risks, and it deserves its own part.

KYC·ON-RAMP· OFF-RAMP·P2P· SETTLEMENT TIME·SPREAD· KYC·ON-RAMP
01 · WHAT AN ON-RAMP ACTUALLY IS

The one door between your bank and your wallet.

An on-ramp is any service that converts fiat currency, dollars, naira, whatever you're starting with, into crypto. Centralized exchanges, CEXs from Part 08, are the most common on-ramp, letting you deposit fiat directly through a bank transfer or card, buy an asset, then withdraw it to your own non custodial wallet from Part 02.

The moment you deposit fiat into a CEX, you're back in the custodial model, someone else holding your funds until you withdraw. That's not a flaw, it's simply the necessary bridge between a banking system that requires identity and a chain that doesn't. Know exactly when you cross that line, and get your assets into self custody as soon as the on-ramp step is done.

02 · KYC, THE PRICE OF ENTRY

Permissionless on chain. Permissioned at the door.

Part 01's entire thesis rests on permissionless value transfer. That permissionless property starts the moment your funds land on chain, not before. Getting there almost always requires KYC, know your customer, identity verification most regulated exchanges are legally required to collect before letting you convert fiat.

This isn't a contradiction of the thesis, it's the actual boundary of it. The chain itself doesn't ask who you are. The regulated on-ramp connecting it to the banking system does. Understanding exactly where that boundary sits prevents a lot of confused frustration for newcomers expecting the entire stack to be anonymous.

03 · OFF-RAMPS, GETTING BACK TO SPENDABLE MONEY

The exit matters as much as the entry.

An off-ramp reverses the process, converting crypto back into fiat you can actually spend or hold in a bank account. Same exchanges usually handle both directions. The details that actually matter here, settlement time, how long the fiat takes to actually land in your bank after you sell, and the spread, the gap between the market price and what you actually receive, which varies significantly between platforms and isn't always advertised clearly upfront.

Test any new off-ramp with a small amount before routing real size through it. Confirm settlement time and final amount received match what was advertised, before you trust it with something that actually matters.

04 · PEER TO PEER RAILS, THE ALTERNATIVE PATH

When the regulated path doesn't reach where you are.

In markets where banking infrastructure or regulated exchange access is limited or unreliable, peer to peer platforms let individuals trade fiat for crypto directly with each other, with the platform typically providing escrow and dispute resolution rather than acting as a counterparty itself. This is a real, widely used rail across large parts of the world where the standard CEX on-ramp path is thin or absent.

The tradeoff is counterparty risk shifts from an institution to an individual. Use platforms with real reputation systems and enforced escrow, verify the counterparty's trade history before committing to a trade, and never release funds or confirm payment outside the platform's own escrow and dispute system.

05 · CHOOSING AN ON-RAMP, THE ACTUAL CRITERIA

Not the platform with the best ad. The one with the best receipts.

→
Check actual regulatory standing. A licensed, regulated exchange in your jurisdiction carries real legal recourse if something goes wrong. An unregulated platform doesn't.
→
Check the real total cost. Advertised fees rarely include the spread. Compare the actual amount of crypto received for a fixed fiat amount across two or three platforms before committing.
→
Check withdrawal limits and verification tiers. Some platforms cap withdrawals until deeper KYC tiers are completed, which can strand funds at the worst possible moment if you haven't verified in advance.
The chain doesn't ask who you are. The door to it usually does. Know exactly where that line sits.
