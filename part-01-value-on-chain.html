Most guides open with the ledger. Wrong order. Nobody wakes up curious about distributed consensus. They wake up because they heard money can move without a bank standing in the middle of it. 01 · WHAT A TOKEN ACTUALLY IS

It's not a coin. It's an entry.

Forget the word crypto for a moment. A token is a balance recorded inside a piece of software that everyone running the network agrees to trust, because the rules for changing that balance are public and enforced by code, not by a person at a desk.

When you "own" a token, you don't own a file. You control the only key that can authorize a change to your entry in that ledger. That's the entire mechanic. No vault, no teller, no institution deciding whether your request is allowed. The network checks the math and either accepts the change or rejects it.

This is the part people skip past to get to the price chart. Don't. If you understand this one sentence, you understand more than most people who have been in this space for two years.

Two categories matter early. Fungible tokens are interchangeable, one unit is identical to the next, same as currency. Non fungible tokens are unique entries, each one distinct. We go deep on the second category in Part 06. Right now, focus on the first, because it's the one that actually moves value at scale.

02 · NATIVE ASSETS VS CONTRACT TOKENS

Two different jobs, one word.

Every chain has a native asset. ETH on Ethereum. SOL on Solana. INJ on Injective. XRP on the XRP Ledger. ADA on Cardano. This is the asset that pays for the network's own security and computation. It existed before any application was built on top of the chain, and the chain cannot function without it.

Then there are contract tokens. Built on top of a chain using its programming standard. ERC20 on Ethereum. SPL on Solana. These are applications, not infrastructure. A project launches one when it wants its own unit of account, its own incentive design, its own economy sitting on top of someone else's security.

L1Native asset secures the base chain
L2Contract token secures nothing on its own
1:1Every contract token depends on its host chain staying alive

Why this distinction matters practically: if the chain goes down, every contract token on it goes down with it. When you evaluate a project, you're never just evaluating the token. You're evaluating the chain underneath it, whether you asked to or not.

03 · GAS, THE PRICE OF TRUTH

Nothing moves for free. That's the point.

Gas is the fee you pay to have the network execute your transaction. Not a platform fee. Not profit for a company. Payment to the validators and miners who do the computation and secure the result.

Here's why it exists at all. Block space is finite. A block can only hold so many transactions. If sending a transaction were free, the network would flood instantly, spam and legitimate activity indistinguishable, the whole system unusable. Gas is what keeps a blockchain from collapsing under its own openness. It's a toll, not a tax.

Gas is priced by demand for that block space. More people trying to transact at once, higher the price to get included. This is why fees spike during high activity and drop to nothing at 4am. You're not paying for your transaction's complexity alone, you're paying for your place in the queue.

Chains compete on this number more than on almost anything else. A chain with expensive, unpredictable gas loses builders and users to one with cheap, predictable gas. This is a large part of why "which chain" became a real question instead of a solved one.

Every ecosystem prices it differently. Ethereum runs a fee market with a burn mechanism, base fee destroyed, priority fee tipped to validators. Solana keeps fees near flat and low by design, optimizing for throughput over auction dynamics. Injective runs near zero fees for most user actions, subsidizing the cost at the protocol level because the chain was built for trading activity that can't tolerate friction. Same problem, three different answers. We map this properly in Part 04.

04 · STABLECOINS, THE ONE THING THAT WORKED

The only product web3 has fully proven.

Say what you want about the rest of this industry. Stablecoins work. A token designed to hold a fixed value, almost always pegged one to one with the US dollar, moving on public rails instead of banking rails. That's not a narrative. That's billions in daily settlement, functioning, right now.

Three models, and the difference between them is the difference between safe and catastrophic.

Fiat collateralized. USDC, USDT. A company holds real dollars or short term treasuries in reserve, one for one against every token issued. The trust here is not in code. It's in the issuer's honesty and the auditors checking their books. Centralized trust wearing a decentralized outfit. Know that going in.

Crypto collateralized. DAI is the reference example. Backed by a basket of onchain assets locked in smart contracts, overcollateralized to absorb price swings in the collateral itself. More decentralized, more transparent, more moving parts that can go wrong under extreme volatility.

Algorithmic. No collateral, or minimal collateral, the peg held together by incentive design and a second token absorbing the volatility. This model has a body count. UST, the Terra stablecoin, ran this design and lost its peg in May 2022, erasing roughly forty billion dollars in value in a matter of days. Study that collapse before you touch anything with this model. It's not a cautionary footnote. It's the single clearest lesson in this entire guide.

A peg is a promise. Promises break under pressure. Know which promise you're holding.
05 · WHY THIS IS THE BIGGEST FORM OF ONCHAIN VALUE

Speculation gets the headlines. Settlement does the work.

NFTs get the culture. DeFi yield gets the spreadsheets. Memecoins get the volume on a bad Tuesday. None of it moves the amount of real economic value that stablecoin settlement moves, quietly, every single day, across borders that used to require a correspondent bank and three business days.

That's the actual thesis underneath this whole guide. Not that web3 replaces finance overnight. That it already replaced one specific, boring, enormous function: moving a dollar equivalent from one party to another without asking permission first. Everything else in this series, wallets, chains, DeFi, NFTs, DAOs, is built on top of that one proven fact, or trying to be.

When you evaluate any new protocol, any new chain, any new narrative someone pitches you, come back to this part. Ask what value is actually moving, what it costs to move it, and whether the promise behind it is collateral you can verify or a story you're being asked to trust.
