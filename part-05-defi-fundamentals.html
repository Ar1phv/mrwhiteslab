DeFi Fundamentals

DEXs, lending, liquidity, and how to tell real yield from a Ponzi with a nice interface.

DeFi means decentralized finance. It means the same financial primitives that already exist, trading, lending, borrowing, rebuilt as open code instead of gated institutions. Some of it is genuinely revolutionary. A lot of it is old fraud wearing a new interface. This part teaches you to tell the difference.

01 · DECENTRALIZED EXCHANGES

No order book operator. Just a formula.

A DEX lets you swap one token for another without a company sitting in the middle holding custody of your funds. Most run on an Automated Market Maker model, a pool of two assets, priced against each other by a formula, not by matching individual buyers to sellers.

You trade directly against the pool. The price moves as the ratio inside it shifts. This is why large trades against thin pools cause slippage, the price moving against you mid trade because you're changing the ratio yourself. Check pool depth before sizing a trade, not after.

Some ecosystems run true on chain order books instead of AMMs, Injective's native order book being the clearest example, giving traders limit orders and tighter pricing closer to a traditional exchange, without giving up self custody.

02 · LIQUIDITY PROVISION AND IMPERMANENT LOSS

Earning fees means taking on a real position, not a free lunch.

Deposit both assets in a pair into a pool and you become a liquidity provider, earning a cut of every trade that passes through it. Sounds like passive income. It's not free. When the price of the two assets diverges from each other, the pool automatically rebalances your holdings in a way that leaves you worse off than if you'd simply held the assets separately. This is impermanent loss, and it's permanent the moment you withdraw during a divergence.

Fee income has to outpace impermanent loss for LP positions to actually make sense. Anyone advertising an LP strategy without mentioning this mechanic is either new or not being straight with you.

03 · LENDING AND BORROWING

Overcollateralized by design, because there's no credit score here.

DeFi lending protocols let you deposit an asset and borrow against it, or lend it out to earn interest. Because there's no identity verification and no legal recourse if a borrower disappears, nearly every protocol requires overcollateralization, you can only borrow a fraction of the value you've deposited, keeping the protocol solvent even if prices move against the borrower.

Drop below the required ratio and the position gets liquidated automatically, your collateral sold to repay the loan, usually with a penalty. This isn't a bug, it's the mechanism that makes trustless lending solvent at all. Understand your liquidation price before you borrow, not after the market moves against you.

04 · READING A YIELD NUMBER HONESTLY

High APY is a question, not an answer.

Yield in DeFi comes from real sources, trading fees, lending interest, protocol revenue, or it comes from token emissions, newly printed tokens handed out as rewards, funded by nothing but inflation. Both show up as the same big percentage number on a dashboard. Only one of them is sustainable.

→
Ask where the yield comes from. Real fees and interest, or freshly minted tokens with no buyer demand behind them.
→
Check if the number survives the reward token's price. A 200% APY paid in a token that's down 90% is not actually 200%.
→
Look for a real revenue source. Trading volume, protocol fees, actual usage. If none exists, the yield is being paid by the next depositor's capital.

An unsustainable emissions model paying early depositors with later depositors' capital is structurally a Ponzi, regardless of what the protocol calls itself. The math doesn't care about the branding.

05 · COMPOSABILITY, THE REAL SUPERPOWER

Protocols stacking on protocols, without asking permission.

The genuinely novel part of DeFi isn't any single application. It's composability, the fact that any protocol can build directly on top of any other, permissionlessly, because everything lives as open code on the same public infrastructure. A lending protocol can plug straight into a DEX's liquidity. A yield aggregator can route capital across a dozen protocols automatically, no partnership meetings required.

This is also where risk compounds. Money legos means a failure in one foundational protocol can cascade through everything built on top of it. Evaluate not just the protocol you're using directly, but every protocol it depends on underneath.

If you can't explain where the yield comes from in one sentence, you don't understand the position you're in.
