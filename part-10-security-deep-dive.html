01 · RUG PULLS, THE MECHANICS BEHIND THE WORD

Someone always holds the exit you don't have.

A rug pull happens when a project's team drains the liquidity or treasury and disappears, leaving holders with a token that's suddenly worth nothing because there's no counterparty left to sell to. The mechanics vary, an unlocked liquidity pool the team can withdraw unilaterally, a hidden minting function that lets them print unlimited supply, or admin keys that let them simply change the rules after launch.

→
Check if liquidity is locked. A time locked or burned liquidity pool means the team physically cannot pull it early.
→
Check for admin functions. Contracts with unrestricted mint, pause, or upgrade functions controlled by one wallet are a single point of failure.
→
Check the team's actual track record. Anonymous teams aren't automatically fraudulent, but anonymous teams with no prior shipped work carry meaningfully more risk.
02 · WHAT AN AUDIT ACTUALLY GUARANTEES

Less than most people assume. Still worth checking.

A smart contract audit is a professional review of a protocol's code, looking for vulnerabilities before real funds are at risk. Useful, and worth checking for before depositing meaningful size. Not a guarantee. Audited protocols have still been exploited, because audits find known vulnerability patterns, they don't prove the absence of every possible flaw, and a paid audit from a low quality firm is close to worthless.

Check who performed the audit, read the actual findings and whether they were resolved, not just whether a badge exists on the website. A badge with no linked report behind it is decoration, not proof.

03 · PHISHING INFRASTRUCTURE HAS GOTTEN GOOD

The fake looks identical. Check the parts that can't be faked.

Cloned websites, near identical domain names, fake support accounts replying inside real project Discord servers, and drainer contracts disguised as routine mints or claims, this is a full criminal industry now, with tooling sophisticated enough that visual inspection alone won't catch it. I terminated a community management engagement mid onboarding once a phishing domain was identified in that exact setup, and reported the issue rather than proceeding.

Bookmark official links directly rather than searching or clicking links from DMs, ever. Verify a suspicious support contact through a second, independent channel before acting on anything they tell you. Treat unsolicited DMs offering help as a threat by default, not a courtesy.

04 · OPSEC FOR ANYONE BUILDING A PUBLIC PROFILE

Visibility is leverage. It's also a target painted on your back.

Operating publicly in this industry, as a creator, builder, or operator, adds a layer of risk past the standard wallet security from Part 02. Public wallet addresses tied to a known identity become direct social engineering targets. Fake job offers, fake investment opportunities, and fake urgent messages from "team members" escalate specifically because an attacker knows exactly who you are and what you might have.

Separate your operational wallets from any wallet address you've ever publicly associated with your identity. Assume any DM claiming urgency, opportunity, or authority is hostile until independently verified through a channel the sender doesn't control.

05 · SMART CONTRACT EXPLOITS, THE PATTERNS THAT REPEAT

Most hacks aren't new. They're the same five mistakes, again.

Reentrancy attacks, where a malicious contract calls back into a vulnerable function before its first execution finishes, draining funds through repeated calls. Oracle manipulation, referenced in Part 08, feeding a contract false price data to trigger unfair liquidations or mispriced trades. Flash loan exploits, borrowing enormous uncollateralized sums for a single transaction to manipulate a protocol's internal pricing before repaying the loan in the same block.

None of these require you to understand the code yourself. What they require is checking whether a protocol holding your funds has been audited by a reputable firm, has a track record without major incidents, and hasn't concentrated too much value in a design pattern known to be historically exploited.

Every major loss in this industry had a warning sign that was visible before the money moved. Slow down enough to see it.
